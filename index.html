Your T2125 expenses

You're registered for GST/HST, so the amounts on Form T2125 should leave out any GST/HST you claim back as input tax credits. These totals still include the tax you paid. Subtract your input tax credits before you use them.

Meals and entertainment are generally limited to 50%. Utilities and vehicle costs only count for the business part, so enter your business-use percentage (from your logbook for a vehicle). Keep your receipts.

How it works, and what it won't do

Each T2125 expense line has a handful of example phrases, like "Google Ads" for advertising or "coworking space membership" for rent. When you load a statement, every description and every example is turned into a 384-number vector by all-MiniLM-L6-v2, an open-weight sentence model, using Transformers.js in your browser. A transaction goes to the line whose examples it sits closest to. When you correct a row, that row becomes a new example for close matches, every row from the same merchant moves with it, and the merchant is remembered in this browser for next time. Store numbers and city names are stripped first, so "Marriott Montreal" isn't mistaken for a Montreal restaurant.

It doesn't know your business. A Staples run could be supplies or a new chair (a capital purchase, which goes under capital cost allowance instead), and a phone bill is usually only partly business. It doesn't work out input tax credits, business-use-of-home, or capital cost allowance. Card payments, tax instalments and transfers to savings are set aside as not an expense, and refunds come off the merchant's line. Treat it as a first sort that you check, not a tax return.

How well does it sort? On 186 held-out transactions it put 133 (72%) on the right line, against 92 (49%) for a plain keyword matcher. It flagged 85 rows for a look, and those flags caught 46 of its 53 mistakes. You can run that check yourself.

Sources: CRA, Expenses section of Form T2125 (the line numbers) and CRA Guide T4002, Chapter 3 (the 50% limit on meals and entertainment, and business-use rules for vehicles).
